## Hypothesis

A bot that reads the house auction by replaying the house bidder (softmax likelihood, on the pool of layouts after the opening lead, the scorer still drawing the pool) beats the same bot reading it with the hand-fit scorer by +0.15 IMPs a board or more. Basis: E9-8 (+0.69 nats a hidden seat on Gen 6 auctions, +1.4 for seats that bid twice or more) and the one exchange-rate point we have (about 0.4 IMPs a board for about 1.1 nats of play reading). Prediction, not a known value: the scorer as proposal keeps only 40% to 46% of the replay posterior's mass, and the rate for auction nats may differ from the rate for play nats.

## Mechanism

The bidder is deterministic given the hand type and the calls so far, so replay is the exact reading of its calls; the scorer is a learned approximation of it. Sharper weights on the hidden hands from trick 1 move the search toward the right card. It also removes a dependency: when the bidder is retrained, the reading follows it with nothing to retrain.

## Primary metric and population

Played boards, fork-free, fresh deals: `9r-32` (the Gen 8 prototype `8p-32` plus the replay reader for the common bidder and for each side's own bidder) against `8p-32`, IMPs a board with its standard error, 1,600 boards. Also recorded: the gain in the true layout's weight per decision from the replay term, and CPU seconds a board against `8p-32`'s.

## Regression slices

Declarer against defender decisions. Boards where a hidden seat bid twice or more against boards where the hidden seats only passed. `9r-32` against `7s-32` beside `8p-32` against `7s-32` on the same deals (does the gain add to Gen 8's).

One box for 4 hours; a 200-board pilot in a sandbox does not count.
