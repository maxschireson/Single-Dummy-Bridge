## Hypothesis

A net trained without one bot's decisions predicts that bot worse than a net trained with them, and the gap depends on how far the bot is from the rest of the training mix. Predictions, not known values: (1) leaving out `71s-32` while `7s-32` stays in costs less than 0.03 in log-loss on its decisions; (2) leaving out `8c-32` (Precision rule bidder) or BEN 64 costs more than 0.08; (3) a net trained on house bots only loses more on BEN's decisions than on any house bot's.

## Mechanism

Training data is the nets' only source of how other players behave. `net.who` stores each known bot's habits in its row; a bot outside the training data is read through the generic row, which averages the mix (docs/gen9/arena-selfplay.md, sections 1 and 7.3).

## Primary metric and population

For each left-out bot: log-loss on its decisions in plan's common held-out set (boards after 02:27 UTC on 13 Nov, minus both training sets; `scripts/score_rollout_nets.py`), read with the generic row, minus the same for a net trained on the full mix. Nets: full mix; without `71s-32`; without `8c-32`; without BEN 64; without `5RZ32`; house bots only.

## Regression slices

Tricks 1 to 6 and 7 to 13; declarer and defenders; decisions with a choice only.

## Compute cap

A small-net proxy in the sandbox only if one epoch on one million decisions takes under 30 minutes and the full-mix proxy's log-loss is within 0.05 of v2's on the same decisions; otherwise full-size nets on a training box, which is Max's decision (no box is used before he says so).

## Stopping rule

One net per condition above, one pass. If the proxy fails its check, report that and stop.
