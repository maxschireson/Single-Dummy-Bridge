## Hypothesis

A house bot that follows a designed carding scheme (count, attitude and lead conventions, honesty 1) and whose partner reads it exactly defends better than `8p-32`, whose spot cards are whatever the net's tie-break does. Head to head against `8p-32` (which reads the signaller's cards with the house net) the signalling pair wins by 0.1 to 0.3 IMPs a board. When the opposing declarer is given the scheme and reads the signals too, part of that is taken back. Predictions, not known values.

## Mechanism

Count and attitude tell partner the shape and honours of the hidden hand directly; the layouts a defender searches over get sharper earlier. The same cards inform a declarer who knows the scheme.

## Primary metric and population

Played boards, fork-free, fresh deals, IMPs a board with standard errors, 1,600 boards each:
1. signalling pair (partner reads) against `8p-32`;
2. signalling pair against `8p-32` whose declarer reads the declared scheme;
3. if a box with BEN servers is available: signalling pair against BEN, beside `8p-32` against BEN on the same deals.
Also the gain in the true layout's weight at defenders' decisions with and without partner's signals read.

## Regression slices

Tricks 1 to 6 against later. Suit contracts against no-trump. Standard against upside-down (expected: no difference between the two, each read by its own scheme).

## Compute cap

One box for 4 hours.

## Stopping rule

The fixed samples.

## Result

**Part 1 (prediction, no box): designed signals at honesty 1 gain the house defence +0.13 ± 0.06 nats of the true layout's weight per defender decision over today's house reading, and give a declarer who knows the scheme +0.32 ± 0.11 per declarer decision. In nats the leak is the larger.** Part 2 (played boards) waits for a box.

150 recorded Gen 7 main tables (`arena-gen7-phase1-azure1-feed`), both defenders' spots and leads re-chosen by the scheme within the recorded tie sets; recorded and relabelled games scored at the same plies, tricks 1 to 6; 1,399 defender and 1,555 declarer decisions per cell; standard errors clustered by table. "Today": the recorded spots read by the house net in full.

| Signals | Partner reads them (defender decisions) | A declarer who knows the scheme (the leak) | A declarer reading with the house net |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| All (fourth-best leads, attitude on partner's lead, count on declarer's, attitude discards) | +0.130 ± 0.056 | +0.317 ± 0.107 | -0.729 ± 0.078 |
| Attitude on partner's lead only | +0.031 ± 0.031 | +0.030 ± 0.045 | -0.335 ± 0.050 |
| Count on declarer's lead only | +0.065 ± 0.026 | +0.179 ± 0.047 | -0.241 ± 0.043 |
| Attitude discards only | -0.009 ± 0.020 | -0.037 ± 0.030 | -0.153 ± 0.031 |
| Fourth-best leads only | -0.018 ± 0.045 | -0.039 ± 0.077 | -0.387 ± 0.063 |

- Which signal carries it: count (defence +0.065 ± 0.026, leak +0.18 ± 0.05). Attitude alone is small on both sides; discards and conventional leads add nothing (the house net already reads the house bots' lead habit, E9-9).
- A declarer who reads these spots with the house net in full, as `8p-32` does, is misled (-0.73 nats a decision). So part 2's first comparison (`cp-std-100~8p-32`) mixes partner's information with misleading an uncalibrated opponent; the comparison against `r9p-32` (a declarer told the scheme) isolates the signals' value. Added for part 2: `cp-att-100~r9p-32`, attitude only (plan: `docs/gen9/signals-runs.md` on `feature/gen9-signals-v2`).
- Evidence: prediction; part 2 not yet run.
- Sample: 150 tables; all signals +0.130 ± 0.056 nats a defender decision, +0.317 ± 0.107 leaked a declarer decision (1 se).
- Revisit if: E9-0's exchange rate shows a defender's nat is worth much more than a declarer's (then the gain can outweigh the leak), or partner is made to act on the signals (here the recorded play stands).
