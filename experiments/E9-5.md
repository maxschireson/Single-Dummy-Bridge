## Hypothesis

(a) The rule in use by hand today (promote when a 95% interval on the direct match excludes zero after at least 1,000 boards, looked at every 100 boards up to 5,000) promotes a candidate that is exactly equal to the champion clearly more often than 5% of the time. (b) The always-valid test of G9-A4 keeps that at or below 5% and needs at most 1.7 times the boards of a single fixed-sample test to detect a true lead of 0.1 to 0.2 IMPs a board. (c) A schedule that sends boards to the pairs with the widest intervals reaches a rating table of a given precision in fewer boards than equal shares.

Predictions, not known values.

## Mechanism

Looking repeatedly at a fixed-level interval gives chance many tries. An always-valid interval pays for the looks in width. Boards on pairs whose difference is already known buy little.

## Primary metric and population

Simulated arenas of 8 to 20 players with true strengths spread like the published results (Gen 1 to Gen 7 and BEN), paired-board noise drawn from the real feeds' per-board IMP differences, arrivals and departures. False promotion rate at a true gap of 0; power and median boards to a decision at gaps of 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4 IMPs a board; root-mean-square rating error against boards played. 2,000 simulated runs a setting. Kind of evidence: simulation.

## Regression slices

With a pair effect added (a pair that beats the model's prediction by 0.3). With one player whose noise is twice the others'. With the noise taken from near-equal bots (about 2.6 IMPs) and from unequal ones (about 3.8).
