## Hypothesis

(a) The rule in use by hand today (promote when a 95% interval on the direct match excludes zero after at least 1,000 boards, looked at every 100 boards up to 5,000) promotes a candidate that is exactly equal to the champion clearly more often than 5% of the time. (b) The always-valid test of G9-A4 keeps that at or below 5% and needs at most 1.7 times the boards of a single fixed-sample test to detect a true lead of 0.1 to 0.2 IMPs a board. (c) A schedule that sends boards to the pairs with the widest intervals reaches a rating table of a given precision in fewer boards than equal shares.

Predictions, not known values.

## Mechanism

Looking repeatedly at a fixed-level interval gives chance many tries. An always-valid interval pays for the looks in width. Boards on pairs whose difference is already known buy little.

## Primary metric and population

Simulated arenas of 8 to 20 players with true strengths spread like the published results (Gen 1 to Gen 7 and BEN), paired-board noise drawn from the real feeds' per-board IMP differences, arrivals and departures. False promotion rate at a true gap of 0; power and median boards to a decision at gaps of 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4 IMPs a board; root-mean-square rating error against boards played. 2,000 simulated runs a setting. Kind of evidence: simulation.

## Regression slices

With a pair effect added (a pair that beats the model's prediction by 0.3). With one player whose noise is twice the others'. With the noise taken from near-equal bots (about 2.6 IMPs) and from unequal ones (about 3.8).

### Sharpened by dave before the first run (12 Nov)

- **Tests compared.** (1) The hand rule: from 1,000 boards, every 100 boards to 5,000, promote when
  the lower end of a two-sided 95% t-interval on the direct match is above zero; no decision at
  5,000. (2) An always-valid interval with a running variance (normal-mixture boundary with the
  sample variance plugged in, two-sided level 0.05, first look at 200 boards). (3) An
  empirical-Bernstein always-valid interval for values bounded in [-24, 24] (predictable plug-in,
  two-sided level 0.05). Both (2) and (3) promote when the lower end passes zero and stop for
  futility when the upper end falls below zero; cap 40,000 boards. G9-A4's exact forms replace
  mine when they land; any run made before that is labelled with the form used.
- **Fixed-sample comparison for (b).** The size of one fixed-sample test with one-sided level
  0.025 and the same power as the sequential test at that gap, from the noise's own SD; the ratio
  is median boards to a decision over that size.
- **Noise sources.** Gaussian at SD 2.6, 3.2 and 4.2; and bootstraps of centred per-board values
  (common auction, dashboard counting rules) from three pools: twin (71s-32~7s-32, plus g8's
  fork-free 7s-v41~7s-32 match if its rows can be fetched), sibling (7s-32~6-32, 6-32~6-256),
  unrelated (pairs against BEN, Gen 5, Gen 3, Gen 2 and Gen 1). The true gap is added to the
  centred values.
- **Schedule (c).** Arenas of 8, 12 and 20 players with strengths drawn from the published
  spread (Gen 1 to Gen 7, BEN), lineage tags deciding a pair's noise pool, epochs of 2,000 boards,
  an arrival and a departure every second epoch. Strategies: equal shares over all pairs; equal
  shares over champion-and-anchor pairs; boards to the pairs that most reduce the summed variance
  of the fitted ratings. Ratings fitted by weighted least squares on pair means with per-pair
  variance, scale fixed by sum to zero. Metric: root-mean-square error of active players' fitted
  strengths against the truth, by boards played.

## Compute cap

2 CPU-hours in a sandbox.

## Stopping rule

The fixed number of runs.

## Result

Kind of evidence: simulation. Report, data and code: `reports/gen9-arena-sim-2026-11-12/README.md` on
`feature/gen9-arena-sim` at `bd1b81b` (PR #17). Noise: bootstraps of 3,439 twin, 4,216 sibling and 12,297
unrelated per-board values from the published feeds and g8's fork-free v4.1-against-v4 match, and Gaussian
at SD 2.6, 3.2 and 4.2. 2,000 runs a promotion setting.

- **(a) Confirmed.** The hand rule promotes an equal candidate in 9.5% to 12.4% of runs (standard error
  about 0.007).
- **(b) G9-A4's betting test:**
  - **Level.** An equal candidate is promoted in 1.5% to 3.5% of runs at caps of 3,000 to 20,000.
  - **Futility.** A candidate truly leading by d is stopped in 4.7% to 7.7% of runs.
  - **Boards.** It needs 1.46 to 1.76 times a fixed-sample test's boards at leads of 0.1 and 0.2. The
    prediction "at most 1.7" holds in 4 of the 5 cells reached, and fails for sibling noise at 0.1 (1.76).
  - **Other rules.** The normal-mixture interval needs 1.62 to 1.91 times; empirical Bernstein needs 2.4
    to 2.8.
- **(c) Supported, with a qualification.** Variance-directed scheduling reaches an RMSE of 0.08 in fewer
  boards than equal shares at 12 players (12,500 against 15,871) and at 20 players (23,842, against not
  within 30,000). It ends lower in every arena. It is no better at 0.10 with 8 players and slightly worse
  after the first epoch. 200 runs a schedule setting, not 2,000; the standard error of the mean RMSE was
  already about 0.002.
- **G9-A4 section 5, further:**
  - **Cap and d.** d = 0.1 ends equal candidates a third sooner at unchanged power for leads of 0.1 and 0.2.
  - **Displayed interval.** At leads of 0.1 and 0.2 it still includes zero at 64% to 79% of promotions,
    and it includes d at nearly every futility stop.
  - **Coverage.** Rating-table coverage is 95% to 97% except for a pair with a real 0.3 pair effect, whose
    cell is covered 67% (equal design) or 85% (sparse).
  - **Gain rule.** It matches the direct schedule for a field score, and its reported SE is calibrated.

Proposed outcome (the reviewer's call): **adopted** as evidence for A4's defaults, with three proposed changes:
1. d = 0.1 and a cap of 10,000 or more.
2. Show the betting state instead of the asymptotic interval.
3. Flag out-of-line pairs by their leave-one-out residual.
