## Hypothesis

For a partnership whose bidder we hold, replaying the bidder on each candidate hand ("would this hand have made these calls") reads a seat's calls at least 1 nat better than the hand-fit scorer does, in the true hand's weight over a uniform pool, on house Gen 6 and Gen 5 auctions. Prediction, not a known value: the scorer was trained toward exactly this indicator, so the gap may be small.

## Mechanism

The house bidders are deterministic given the hand and the calls so far, and their inputs are exactly the hand type (points, suit lengths, A K Q J T, and the number of low cards per suit). So the exact posterior over a seat's hand given its calls is uniform over the types that would have made them, and the scorer is a learned approximation of that set. Replay is the scorer's ceiling; the same replay reads any profile whose generator we hold (house nets, BEN's bidder, rule bidders, compiled cards).

## Primary metric and population

Gain in nats of the true hand's weight over a uniform pool, as in `reports/gen9-filter-prelim-2026-11-12` (one seat seen by its left-hand opponent, pool of 256; then the registered E9-1 protocol with 512 layouts from the leader's and declarer's views). Auctions bid at all four seats by the house Gen 6 bidder (the arena's) and by Gen 5; 1,600 seat decisions each. Readers: scorer v0; replay as an indicator with `eps` 0.02 and 0.1; replay with the bidder's own softmax as the likelihood of each call; and the two-stage form the engine would use (pool of 512 drawn by the scorer, re-weighted by replay over scorer). Also the cost: bidder steps and seconds per board for a pool of 512 and two hidden seats, with a cache by hand type. Kind of evidence: prediction.

## Regression slices

By the number of calls the seat made (a seat that only passed against one that bid twice). Auctions of four calls or fewer against longer ones. Whether the true hand ever fails its own replay (it must not: a test).

## Compute cap

2 CPU-hours in a sandbox.

## Stopping rule

The fixed samples.

## Result

**12 November 2026, bill.** One-seat protocol (each seat read by its left-hand opponent over 256 uniform random hands plus the true one), 400 deals and 1,600 decisions per bidder, all four seats bid by the house bidder.

| Auctions by | S | replay argmax eps .02 | eps .1 | replay softmax | S + replay softmax |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Gen 6 | 2.13 ± .04 | 2.56 ± .04 | 1.98 ± .03 | 2.82 ± .06 | 2.93 ± .08 |
| Gen 5 | 2.16 ± .04 | 2.53 ± .04 | 1.96 ± .03 | 2.75 ± .06 | 2.86 ± .07 |

Replay with the bidder's softmax beats the scorer by 0.69 (Gen 6) and 0.60 (Gen 5) nats; for seats that bid twice or more by 1.4 (3.16 to 4.62 on Gen 6); for seats that only passed by 0.08. The true hand never fails its own replay (0 of 3,200). Cost 0.030 s per seat over a pool of 257 against 0.004 for the scorer, once per hidden seat per board. The scorer as a proposal for 512 layouts keeps 40% (Gen 6) / 46% (Gen 5) of the replay posterior's mass. The registered-protocol run (512 layouts, leader and declarer) is not done yet.

Evidence: prediction (true-hand weight over a uniform pool; no solver).
Sample: 1,600 seat decisions per bidder; standard errors as shown.
Revisit if: the pool is drawn by replay rather than by the scorer, or the registered E9-1 protocol (layouts from the leader's and declarer's views) gives a different size.

Verdict proposed: **adopted** as the reading of any profile whose generator we hold (softmax likelihood, the scorer only as a fallback or a proposal), with the hypothesis's 1 nat met only for seats that bid twice or more (+0.6 to 0.7 overall). Data and code: `reports/gen9-e9-8-2026-11-12/`, `scripts/gen9_e9_8.py` on `feature/gen9-bidding-cards`.
