## Hypothesis

Most of the variance in paired-board IMPs comes from boards where the two tables reach different contracts and the result turns on the layout. Re-scoring each table's contract by its double-dummy expectation over layouts consistent with the auction (declarer's view) removes much of that variance. Predictions, not known values: on native boards where the two tables' contracts differ, the per-board standard deviation falls by at least 30%; on every pair whose plain mean is more than 2 SE from zero, the re-scored mean has the same sign.

## Mechanism

A decision taken at one table and not the other (a slam on a finesse) is scored on the one layout dealt; its expected value over the layouts the players could not tell apart is what the decision earned. This is the cheap form of AIVAT (Burch et al. 2018) for bridge; it is biased because it replaces the play of the re-scored contracts by double dummy (docs/gen9/arena-selfplay.md, section 5.4).

## Primary metric and population

Per pair: standard deviation and standard error of the mean, plain and re-scored; the shift in the mean. Boards: native boards of the registered arena feeds (records with deals and auctions in `arena/replays/data`), the cohort's (`arena-gen9-azure4-feed`) first, split by whether the two tables' contracts differ. E9-0's matches are common auctions (same contract at both tables), so their variance is all card play; they wait for the play version (a correction at the tricks where the two tables' plays part).

## Regression slices

Same contract at both tables against different contracts; game and slam swings; competitive auctions; boards of 10 IMPs or more.

## Compute cap

64 layouts per table from the auction samplers already in use for reading, solved with DDS; at most 2 CPU-hours in the sandbox.

## Stopping rule

One pass over the stated boards. Proposed as a screen if the standard deviation falls by 30% or more and no sign disagrees as stated; otherwise reported and stopped. The unbiased version is a separate experiment, opened only if this one shows the variance is worth chasing.

## Result

Cheap version, run 13 Nov 11:55 to 12:32 UTC in g9's sandbox on the 2,322 native boards of `arena-gen8-azure1-feed` and `arena-gen8-x3-azure1-feed` (`reports/gen9-e9-19-2026-11-13` on `feature/gen9`, ccca305). 439 boards (19%) had different contracts at the two tables, all against an older generation (`1`, `5RZ32`) or `8c-32`; no pair among `8s-32`, `8x3-32` and `71s-32`, and no board of the Gen 9 cohort, ever bid differently (all bid with house-gen6).
- Prediction 1 (SD falls 30% or more where contracts differ): 7.32 to 5.15 IMPs, a fall of 29.6%: not met (23% against `8c-32`, 38% against Gen 1 and Gen 5). Over all boards the SD fell 12%.
- Prediction 2 (pairs beyond 2 SE keep their sign): 7 of 7: met.
- Bias: means move up to 1.7 IMPs a board on the re-scored boards (double-dummy play replaces the pairs' play there); `8s-32` against Gen 1 +2.61 to +1.71 over all its boards.
- Stopping rule: reported and stopped; the unbiased version is not opened.

Evidence: played boards (recorded native results), re-scored by double dummy over weighted layouts (64 a table, effective sample median 53).
Sample: 2,322 boards, 439 re-scored; SD 7.32 against 5.15 on the re-scored boards, 4.65 against 4.10 over all.
Revisit if: the arena compares bots that bid differently (foreign systems, BEN) and needs faster ratings there, or a play version (the trick where the two tables' plays part) is built: that is the only kind that reaches the cohort's variance.
