## Hypothesis

A small per-player head on the policy net, fitted on one player's own recorded cards, reads that player's choices (which class of card: win, duck, cover, switch, which suit) better than the house net at that player's best factor, and the gain grows with the number of boards of experience. For BEN as declarer (the largest record we have): at 2,000 tables of experience the head gains +0.10 nats or more per house defender decision over the factor alone (Gen 8's +0.60 at 0.3, E9-7), and it is ahead by 200 tables. Predictions, not known values; E9-7's own prediction that the factor would carry most of the gain was wrong, so this one is uncertain in both directions.

## Mechanism

Max's third request for Gen 9: an experience base that fine-tunes the play inference, "this player might have done this". Today experience sets one number per player for choices (the factor on the net's class term) and an emission table for spots. A factor can only say "trust the house net less"; it cannot say what this player does instead. The head is the cheapest thing that can: the net's last hidden layer is kept, and a player-specific correction to the output (a low-rank or diagonal change, or a bias by class of card and situation) is fitted by maximum likelihood on the player's recorded decisions, shrunk toward no change.

## Primary metric and population

Offline, by the method of E9-7 and on its split (deal families; 300 held-out tables per BEN version): gain in the true layout's log weight per house decision when BEN's plays are read by (a) the house net at its best factor, (b) the same with the head, for BEN as declarer and BEN as defender (class term; spots stay with the emission table). Reported as a curve over the experience used to fit the head: 50, 200, 1,000 and all tables, 5 random subsets at each size below all.

## Regression slices

BEN 16 and BEN 64 separately. A head fitted on one read on the other (is it a model of BEN or of one setting). The same head fitted on a house bot's own boards must change nothing (within 0.02 nats): the control for overfitting.

## Compute cap

A sandbox, 3 hours. No box.

## Stopping rule

The fixed design. If the head is within noise of the factor at all sizes, say so and stop: the scalar factor is then the right amount of per-player play model for now.

## Result

**A per-player head on the net (526 numbers: a temperature, suit and rank preferences, a reweighting of the decoder's 256 units), fitted on BEN's own recorded choices, reads BEN +0.08 (BEN 16) and +0.14 (BEN 64) nats a house defender decision better than the net at its best factor, and its class term lets BEN's defence be read at factor 1 (+0.13 to +0.19 nats over E9-7's reading). In tricks on the reading bench it is worth +0.035 ± 0.014 (BEN 64) and +0.013 ± 0.012 (BEN 16) a board on BEN's declarer play, and nothing measurable on its defence. It is at its limit by about 1,000 tables; tables are not what limits it.**

- Held-out log-likelihood of BEN's choices over the net, nats a decision, at 50 / 200 / 1,000 / all tables: declarer +0.27 / +0.37 / +0.435 / +0.438 (BEN 16), +0.35 / +0.45 / +0.518 / +0.525 (BEN 64); defenders' classes +0.09 / +0.14 / +0.169 / +0.170 and +0.11 / +0.15 / +0.183 / +0.186. A head fitted on one version reads the other as well as its own.
- Reading gain at the house bots' decisions (E9-7's pools, tricks 1-6), nats: BEN declaring, net at 0.5 +0.018 / +0.001 over 0.3, head at 0.5 +0.100 ± 0.022 / +0.140 ± 0.022, at 1 +0.084 / +0.149; BEN defending, head's class at 1 +0.194 ± 0.038 / +0.132 ± 0.044 over E9-7's reading (the net's class at 1: +0.049 / -0.057).
- Curve, head at 0.5 on the declarer play: +0.047 / +0.064 / +0.097 / +0.100 (BEN 16), +0.056 / +0.104 / +0.137 / +0.140 (BEN 64).
- Bench, tricks a board (300 tables a version, all house decisions after the lead): head on BEN's declarer play at 0.5 against the net at 0.3 +0.013 ± 0.012 / +0.035 ± 0.014; head's class at 1 on BEN's defenders against E9-7's reading +0.002 ± 0.024 / -0.029 ± 0.027; both heads at 1 against Gen 8 +0.030 ± 0.028 / +0.006 ± 0.033. Gen 8 reads BEN for +0.052 / +0.166 a board against no reading, the house net reads the house bots for +0.185.
- Data (tricks 1-6, choices with more than one card): BEN 16 12,608 declarer and 11,940 defender decisions on 1,180 and 1,129 fit tables; BEN 64 15,650 and 15,299 on 1,461 and 1,451; about 150,000 BEN decisions over all tricks. A box of eight BEN lanes made of the order of 1,000 tables an hour.
- Max's question (enough BEN data to train on its card play?): for a head of this size, yes; it stops improving at about 1,000 tables. Whether a model with more capacity (fine-tuning the decoder or more on BEN's decisions) reads more is not settled by this; not started.
- Control: a head fitted on the house defenders reading partner +0.023 ± 0.011 / +0.012 ± 0.011 nats (temperature 1.26 to 1.32: the net is too flat for the house bots as well).
- Against the predictions: +0.10 nats or more at all tables over the factor, BEN 64 yes (+0.14), BEN 16 just short (+0.08); ahead by 200 tables, yes; cross-version, as good as its own; control within 0.02, at the edge.
- Evidence: prediction (held-out log-likelihood; true layout's log weight; double-dummy tricks saved on stored solves), held-out deal families. Report: reports/gen9-play-head-2026-11-12 (feature/gen9-signals-v2, ecd8946).
- Sample: fit on 1,129 to 1,461 tables a version and role, 5 random subsets at 50, 200 and 1,000; scored on E9-7's 300 held-out tables a version (1,381 / 1,400 house defender and 1,582 / 1,570 house declarer decisions, tricks 1-6; bench 6,036 / 6,070 house decisions); 1 se clustered by table (nats) or deal (tricks).
- Revisit if: a larger model of BEN's play (more of the net fine-tuned) is tried, or the BEN lanes run again (a head fitted then can be checked on new tables), or a played match against BEN with the head is scheduled (prediction: about +0.05 to +0.13 IMPs a board from the declarer-play head alone).
