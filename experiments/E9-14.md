## Hypothesis

A small per-player head on the policy net, fitted on one player's own recorded cards, reads that player's choices (which class of card: win, duck, cover, switch, which suit) better than the house net at that player's best factor, and the gain grows with the number of boards of experience. For BEN as declarer (the largest record we have): at 2,000 tables of experience the head gains +0.10 nats or more per house defender decision over the factor alone (Gen 8's +0.60 at 0.3, E9-7), and it is ahead by 200 tables. Predictions, not known values; E9-7's own prediction that the factor would carry most of the gain was wrong, so this one is uncertain in both directions.

## Mechanism

Max's third request for Gen 9: an experience base that fine-tunes the play inference, "this player might have done this". Today experience sets one number per player for choices (the factor on the net's class term) and an emission table for spots. A factor can only say "trust the house net less"; it cannot say what this player does instead. The head is the cheapest thing that can: the net's last hidden layer is kept, and a player-specific correction to the output (a low-rank or diagonal change, or a bias by class of card and situation) is fitted by maximum likelihood on the player's recorded decisions, shrunk toward no change.

## Primary metric and population

Offline, by the method of E9-7 and on its split (deal families; 300 held-out tables per BEN version): gain in the true layout's log weight per house decision when BEN's plays are read by (a) the house net at its best factor, (b) the same with the head, for BEN as declarer and BEN as defender (class term; spots stay with the emission table). Reported as a curve over the experience used to fit the head: 50, 200, 1,000 and all tables, 5 random subsets at each size below all.

## Regression slices

BEN 16 and BEN 64 separately. A head fitted on one read on the other (is it a model of BEN or of one setting). The same head fitted on a house bot's own boards must change nothing (within 0.02 nats): the control for overfitting.

## Compute cap

A sandbox, 3 hours. No box.

## Stopping rule

The fixed design. If the head is within noise of the factor at all sizes, say so and stop: the scalar factor is then the right amount of per-player play model for now.
