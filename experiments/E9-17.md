Hypothesis: a reader told the rule (an emission table that puts the card at the top, or the bottom, of its class whatever the hand's count; for t-random an even choice) beats a reader that only scales the net (8x-32: factors 0.2, 0.5, 0.25) and one that reads the net in full (8p-32, which g8 measured at -0.36 +- 0.13 IMPs against t-high relative to the scaled reader). ## Hypothesis

A reader told a stand-in's fixed rule beats one that only scales the net. The rule is written as an emission table: t-high plays the highest card of its class whatever the hand's count or attitude, t-low the lowest, t-random any card evenly. Against t-high and t-low, `r9t-32` gains +0.1 IMPs a board or more over `8x-32`. Against t-random, `r9t-32` equals `8x-32`, and both beat `8p-32`. These are predictions from the experiment file (g9), not measured values.

## Mechanism

`r9t-32` is `8x-32`, with the same Gen 7 settings and the same factors by opponent type (t-high 0.2, t-low 0.5, t-random 0.25), plus Gen 9's signal reader. Each stand-in has a model in the reader's `models`: a carding block whose descriptor names the four spot situations, with an emission table for each:
- t-high: P(low, high, middle) = (0, 1, 0) for every message;
- t-low: (1, 0, 0);
- t-random: no emission table; every situation declared `none` (see below).

So the reader reads the class of a hidden defender's spot by the net, at the same factor as `8x-32`, and reads the card within the class by the rule. `8x-32` reads the card itself by the net at that factor, and `8p-32` reads it by the net at full weight. Declarer's and dummy's cards, honours, the opening lead (no lead fields in the descriptor) and the auction are read exactly as `8x-32` reads them; for the auction this is checked on two tables (equal centred log weights). Correction before any result, from sam's note of 17:22 UTC: the rule is read only at the four first-round situations. A defender's other spot plays are read as an even choice inside their class, with the class by the net at the same factor; these are the second and later rounds of a suit, discards after the first, and leads after the opening lead. So `r9t-32` differs from `8x-32` in two ways: the rule at the four situations, and an even choice inside the class at the other spot plays, where `8x-32` reads the card itself by the net. The result reports both, as one difference. sam's check, reported beside the result: how often t-high's card is the highest of its class (E9-3's class can be larger than the tie set), measured on r9t-32~t-high's records with scripts/gen9_house_carding.py. t-random: an even table (1/3, 1/3, 1/3) is even only for classes of two and three spots, and `none` is not allowed for two of the four situation fields. Stated (18:22 UTC, before its matches, 5c79898): t-random's block declares no signal in any of the four situations. On sam's advice the fields file now allows `none` for `signal_on_partner_lead` and `first_discard`. Every defender's spot is therefore read as an even choice in its class at every class size, with the class by the net at 0.25.

## Primary metric and population

The primary metric is native IMPs a board from the reader's side: `r9t-32~t-high` minus `8x-32~t-high`, paired by deal. The deals are the same gen8_match deals for all three readers (one seed, indices 0 to 399). The standard error is by deal over the 400 paired deals. Also reported:
- `r9t-32` minus `8p-32`, and `8x-32` minus `8p-32`;
- each match's own mean against t-high;
- the common regime beside the native one.

The same follows for t-low, then t-random, as time allows.

## Regression slices

- Declaring and defending boards separately: the rule only reads defenders' spots, so it can only act when the reader declares, and the defending boards are the check that nothing else moved.
- Boards where the auction differs between the readers' tables. There should be none; the readers bid alike.
