## Hypothesis

When a defender follows suit or discards holding two or more cards ranked nine or below in the suit played, those cards have the same double-dummy value in the true layout in at least 95% of decisions, and the exceptions cluster in situations a simple rule can name (a nine or eight after the higher cards have gone, late tricks). If the share is below 90% the class rule of architecture 4.3 needs changing before the signal model is built on it.

## Mechanism

A signal is a choice among cards that take the same tricks. The class rule assumes low spots are such cards.

## Primary metric and population

Share of such decisions in which the spots do not all have the same double-dummy value on the true deal, and the mean cost in tricks of the worst spot. At least 5,000 defender decisions from the Gen 7 arena records, by trick, by whether following or discarding, by rank of the highest spot held, by cards left in the suit. Kind of evidence: double-dummy proxy.

## Regression slices

Declarer's and dummy's spots, for completeness. Trump suit against side suits.

## Compute cap

2 CPU-hours in a sandbox (solver built locally).

## Stopping rule

The fixed sample.

## Result

**The class rule holds.** Defenders' spots (two to nine) in the suit played do not all take the same number of tricks on the true deal in **1.49% ± 0.08%** of 21,057 decisions (prediction: at most 5%). When they differ, the worst spot costs 1.13 tricks on average; the lowest spot is among the best in 99.2% of all decisions.

- Kind of evidence: double-dummy proxy, true deal. Sample: `arena-gen7-phase1-azure1-feed` at `f9dad79`, all 828 episodes, main tables only; 40,073 decisions in all roles.
- Where the exceptions are (defenders, not leading): top spot a nine 2.82% (5,666), an eight 1.17% (4,006), seven or lower 0.91% or less; trump suit 3.22% (1,861) against 0.91% for side suits at suit contracts and 2.13% at no-trump; no outstanding card above the top spot 4.99% (1,082) against 0.25% with five or more above (4,343). Not late tricks: tricks 10 to 12 are 0.6% to 1.2%. By situation: discarding 0.48%, following partner's lead 1.75%, following declarer's side 1.74%, later leads 1.96%, opening lead 1.00%, ruffs 7.33% (232).
- Other roles: declarer's spots 3.62% (9,478), dummy's 2.03% (9,538).
- The search's ties: in 91.5% of defender spot plays the exactly tied set held the whole class; there the spots differ on the true deal 0.10% of the time, elsewhere (8.5%) 15.5%, and in 57% of those only one spot was tied. A carding bot that signals only among ties therefore avoids almost all costly cases.
- Outcome: **adopted** as the v1 class rule (design note `docs/gen9/signals.md`, section 2). A v1.1 option the data names: a spot with no outstanding card above it is its own class.
- Data and code: branch `research/gen9-spot-equivalence` at `0838808`, `reports/gen9-spot-equivalence-2026-11-12/` (README, tables, rows) and `scripts/gen9_spot_equivalence.py`. Compute: about 4 minutes on two cores, well under the 2 CPU-hour cap.
- Limit: one feed of Gen 7 against Gen 6, 5 and 1, so the positions are those bots' choices of suit.
