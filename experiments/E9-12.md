## Hypothesis

When all four seats bid a system the house scorer never saw (the rule bidder's precision settings), a bot that reads the auction through the declared card (the `card` profile) beats the same bot reading it with the house scorer by a wide margin: +0.5 IMPs a board or more; and reading by replay of the rule bidder itself adds to that. Basis: E9-1 part 1, where on those auctions the scorer puts the true layout 5.8 nats below not reading the auction at all, the card 2.2 above and the exact tables 3.2 above. Prediction, not a known value.

## Mechanism

Reading a strong club as a natural club puts the search's layouts in the wrong place from the first trick. This is the case Gen 9's card reading exists for: a system that arrives with a card and no trained expert.

## Primary metric and population

Played boards, fork-free, fresh deals, the rule bidder (precision) as the common bidder at all four seats: `9c-32` (`8p-32` plus the card profile for the declared card `rb-precision`) against `8p-32`; and `9x-32` (replay of the rule bidder) against `8p-32`. IMPs a board with standard errors, 1,200 boards each. Recorded: the auction's contribution to the true layout's weight per decision under each reader.

## Regression slices

The same pairs with the standard settings as the common bidder (where the scorer is only slightly positive: 0.52 nats). A third bot that does not read the auction at all (uniform), to separate "stop misreading" from "read by the card".

## Compute cap

One box for 5 hours.

## Stopping rule

The fixed samples.
