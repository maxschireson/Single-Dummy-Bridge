## Hypothesis

Against defenders who follow a declared count and attitude scheme with honesty `h`, a declarer whose reader is given the scheme and `h` gains more of the true layout's weight per defender card than the Gen 8 reading of an unknown opponent (the house net at factor 0.3) and than the house net read in full. The gain rises with `h` and equals the generic reading's at `h = 0`. With `h` learned from 200 boards it is within a tenth of the gain with the true `h`. Played out, the reader wins IMPs against the same bot without it when both face the carding bots.

Predictions, not known values.

## Mechanism

A count signal states the parity of a suit and an attitude signal an honour; both are facts about the hidden hands. The house net reads spots by the house bots' habits, which a foreign defender does not share, so today their spots are either misread or discounted.

## Primary metric and population

**Part 1 (prediction).** Gain in log weight of the true layout per declarer decision, by the method of `scripts/gen8_play_info.py`, on boards played against carding bots with full records. Readers: Gen 8 unknown-opponent (factor 0.3); house net in full (factor 1); signal model with the true `h`; signal model with `h` estimated from the first N boards (N = 50, 200, 1,000). Cells: schemes {standard count and attitude, upside-down count and attitude} and lead conventions {fourth best, third and fifth} at `h` in {1.0, 0.8, 0.5, 0}. At least 300 boards a cell.

**Part 2 (played boards; needs a box).** The Gen 8 prototype bot with the signal reader, and the same bot without, each against the same carding bots on the same deals; the difference in IMPs a board with its standard error. At least 1,600 boards for the standard scheme at `h = 0.8`.

## Regression slices

Against house bots with the signal term off (must be identical to the base). Against the random-spot bot (`t-random`; expected: no gain, no loss). By trick: tricks 1 to 4 against later.

## Compute cap

Part 1: boards from about 4 box-hours of matches, analysis in a sandbox. Part 2: one box for 3 hours.
