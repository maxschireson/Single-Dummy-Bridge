## Hypothesis

Against defenders who follow a declared count and attitude scheme with honesty `h`, a declarer whose reader is given the scheme and `h` gains more of the true layout's weight per defender card than the Gen 8 reading of an unknown opponent (the house net at factor 0.3) and than the house net read in full. The gain rises with `h` and equals the generic reading's at `h = 0`. With `h` learned from 200 boards it is within a tenth of the gain with the true `h`. Played out, the reader wins IMPs against the same bot without it when both face the carding bots.

Predictions, not known values.

## Mechanism

A count signal states the parity of a suit and an attitude signal an honour; both are facts about the hidden hands. The house net reads spots by the house bots' habits, which a foreign defender does not share, so today their spots are either misread or discounted.

## Primary metric and population

**Part 1 (prediction).** Gain in log weight of the true layout per declarer decision, by the method of `scripts/gen8_play_info.py`, on boards played against carding bots with full records. Readers: Gen 8 unknown-opponent (factor 0.3); house net in full (factor 1); signal model with the true `h`; signal model with `h` estimated from the first N boards (N = 50, 200, 1,000). Cells: schemes {standard count and attitude, upside-down count and attitude} and lead conventions {fourth best, third and fifth} at `h` in {1.0, 0.8, 0.5, 0}. At least 300 boards a cell.

**Part 2 (played boards; needs a box).** The Gen 8 prototype bot with the signal reader, and the same bot without, each against the same carding bots on the same deals; the difference in IMPs a board with its standard error. At least 1,600 boards for the standard scheme at `h = 0.8`.

## Regression slices

Against house bots with the signal term off (must be identical to the base). Against the random-spot bot (`t-random`; expected: no gain, no loss). By trick: tricks 1 to 4 against later.

## Compute cap

Part 1: boards from about 4 box-hours of matches, analysis in a sandbox. Part 2: one box for 3 hours.

## Stopping rule

The fixed samples above.

## Result

**Part 1 (prediction, no box): reading the declared scheme is worth +0.49 to +0.96 nats of the true layout's weight per declarer decision over Gen 8's reading of an unknown opponent, at honesty 0.5 to 1; the part that comes from the facts rises with honesty and is zero at honesty 0.** Part 2 (played boards) waits for a box.

Defenders' spots and opening leads on 296 recorded Gen 7 main tables (`arena-gen7-phase1-azure1-feed` at `f9dad79`; house bots defending) were drawn again by the carding bot's rule within the recorded tie set, the two cards' order of play swapped; 11 to 20 swaps of about 1,500 per cell were undone because a trick's winner would change; no table dropped. 3,153 declarer decisions per cell (tricks 1 to 6), pool of 256 layouts, standard errors clustered by table. - Hypothesis checks: the gain rises with h (yes); it equals the generic reading's at h = 0 (the facts' part is exactly 0; the reader still gains +0.33 there, from reading the class at factor 1, +0.12 to +0.16, and from knowing the scheme plays extreme spots, about +0.2); it beats the house net read in full (yes, by +0.9 to +1.2; the house net in full is 0.21 to 0.28 below Gen 8's 0.3 on these defenders). Standard and upside-down read alike; third-and-fifth leads as well as fourth best.
- What a wrong honesty costs: against bots at honesty 0, a reader assuming 0.9 loses 0.86 nats against the right reading; against honest bots, assuming 0.5 loses 0.12, and 0.9 reads slightly better than 1 (the bot signals among its tied cards, the reader assumes the whole class). Full table in the report. Honesty should start low and be learned; "h learned from N boards" is in G9-S5's learning curve (running).
- Honesty learned from N boards (G9-S5, `reports/gen9-reading-gain-2026-11-12/curve.md`): from a prior of 0.5, after 25 to 50 boards (about 100 to 200 signalling occasions) the estimate is within 0.05 of where it settles, and the reading gain on 96 held-out tables is within 0.01 to 0.03 nats of the reader given the true honesty (std-80, ud-80, std-50). Prediction ("within a tenth at 200 boards") met. The estimate settles slightly below nominal (0.72 to 0.78 for the 0.8 bots), which is the effective honesty against the whole class.
- By trick: reader minus Gen 8 for `std-100` is +0.76 in tricks 1 to 4 and +1.21 in tricks 5 and 6.
- Limit: prediction only; nobody reacts to the signals. The class choices are the house bots' own, which favours the class factor of 1; against a foreign player it should be lower.
- Data and code: `feature/gen9-signals-v2` at `5a5c51d`, `reports/gen9-reading-gain-2026-11-12/` (README with both tables, report.json, tables.txt, rows) and `scripts/gen9_reading_gain.py`.
- Evidence: prediction (gain in the true layout's log weight); part 2 (played boards) not yet run.
- Sample: 296 tables, 3,153 declarer decisions per cell, 8 cells; e.g. std-80 reader minus Gen 8 +0.72 ± 0.07 nats (1 se, clustered by table).
- Revisit if: the reader's class term is used against a player whose class choices the net does not know (the class factor of 1 here leans on house class choices), or the bots' carding rule plays middle spots.
- Outcome so far: part 1 supports the hypothesis; the experiment stays open for part 2.
