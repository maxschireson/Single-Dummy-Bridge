## Hypothesis

The probability over profiles computed from a pair's finished boards puts 0.95 on the true system within about 10 boards when the systems are as different as the rule bidder's precision and standard settings, within about 50 for the two house bidders (which differ on about a quarter of deals), and needs hundreds when two cards differ only in the no-trump range. The numbers are predictions.

## Mechanism

Each board multiplies in the likelihood of the pair's calls on their actual hands under each profile. Boards where the systems would bid alike add nothing; the rate of discriminating boards sets the speed.

## Primary metric and population

Boards until the probability of the true system first stays at or above 0.95: median and 90th percentile over 200 random orderings of 1,000 boards, for each of the three system pairs, with `unknown` kept as a candidate throughout. Kind of evidence: prediction.

## Regression slices

The same with a wrong card declared (does detection overrule it, and after how many boards). The probability left on `unknown` when the true system is not among the candidates.

## Compute cap

1 CPU-hour in a sandbox.

## Stopping rule

The fixed samples above.

## Result

**12 November 2026, bill.** Registered protocol: 1,000 boards a pair (999 for the rule bidder), all four seats bid by the true system, North-South's calls scored under each candidate on their actual hands, 200 orderings. Per-call likelihood: deterministic generators (rule bidder, compiled card) `(1 - eps)` on their call plus `eps` uniform; nets `(1 - eps)` softmax plus `eps` uniform; `unknown` = 0.8 x the candidates' average distribution + 0.2 uniform.

| Pair (truth first) | Discriminating boards | Boards to 0.95, median / p90 (eps 0.05) | eps 0.2 | Wrong card declared (prior 0.9), eps 0.05 / 0.2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| rule bidder precision vs standard | 26% | 3 / 8 | 3 / 8 | 3 / 8 ; 4 / 12 |
| house Gen 6 vs Gen 5 | 14% | 20 / 37 | 48 / 72 | 36 / 55 ; 49 / 75 |
| 2/1 card, 1NT 15-17 vs 14-16 | 7.5% | 9 / 29 | 9 / 29 | 9 / 29 ; 18 / 47 |

Against the predictions: precision vs standard as predicted (about 10); house bidders 20 to 50 (predicted about 50); the no-trump-range pair 9 to 29, not hundreds, because the profiles are exact generators and each discriminating board is worth about 6 nats. Real players who deviate, or learned profiles, will be slower; eps 0.2 is a first look. `unknown` when the true system is absent: it takes almost no weight once a few hundred boards are in, and is degenerate for the house pair at eps 0.2. A broad model of "another system" is still to be built.

Kind of evidence: prediction. Data and code: `reports/gen9-e9-2-2026-11-12/`, `scripts/gen9_e9_2.py`, `bridge_harness/cards/match.py` on `feature/gen9-bidding-cards`.

Evidence: prediction (likelihood of calls on finished boards).
Sample: 1,000 boards a pair, 200 orderings; three pairs at eps 0.05 and 0.2.
Revisit if: profiles are learned nets rather than the generators themselves, players deviate from their profile, or a broad `unknown` model exists.

Verdict proposed: **adopted** as the detection method (likelihood of calls on actual hands per profile), with the caveat above on `unknown` and on exact-generator profiles.
