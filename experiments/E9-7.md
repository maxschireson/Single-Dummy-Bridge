## Hypothesis

On held-out boards against BEN, a reading fitted to BEN's own recorded cards gains more of the true layout's weight per decision than the house net at factor 0.3 (Gen 8's setting for an unknown opponent) and at factor 1. Most of the gain comes from the cheapest step (a play factor and floor per role and trick band); a small head or adapter on the policy net fitted on BEN's cards adds less. Predictions, not known values.

## Mechanism

The house net predicts house bots. BEN chooses differently among the cards that matter and among spots; a factor of 0.3 throws away most of what BEN's cards say because the net cannot be trusted on them. A model of BEN can be trusted more.

## Primary metric and population

Gain in log weight of the true layout per house-bot decision (declarer reading BEN's defenders; a defender reading BEN as declarer), by the method of `scripts/gen8_play_info.py`. Boards: the Gen 7 and 7.1 feeds against BEN (`arena-gen7-phase1-azure2-ben16-*-feed`, `arena-gen7-phase1-azure3-ben64-*-feed`, `arena-gen7-1-azure3-ben64-*-feed`), and Gen 6's BEN feeds if their records carry what is needed. Split by deal family, 80% to fit and 20% held out; at least 300 held-out boards.

Readers, in order of cost: house net at factors 0, 0.3, 1; factor and floor fitted per role and trick band; adherence of BEN's spots and leads to the standard schemes (does BEN signal at all); a small head or adapter fitted on BEN's cards with the rest of the net frozen. Kind of evidence: prediction. Played boards against BEN with the fitted model come afterwards and need a box with BEN servers.

## Regression slices

BEN (16) and BEN (64) as one player or two. Boards the 4.1 net trained on are excluded when v4.1 is the reading net (board entry of 06:51 UTC).

## Compute cap

Sandbox for the first three readers (about 3 CPU-hours); the adapter needs a box or a long sandbox run.

## Stopping rule

The fixed split.

## Result

**A model fitted to BEN's own cards reads BEN's defenders +0.16 ± 0.05 (BEN 16) and +0.32 ± 0.05 (BEN 64) nats of the true layout's weight per house declarer decision better than Gen 8 (net at 0.3), on held-out deal families. The gain comes from BEN's count signals, read by an asymmetric emission model, not from tuning the play factor, which was already right.**

- Does BEN signal: yes, by count from a low-card default. On declarer's lead the highest spot is played 0.21 of the time from even length and 0.05 from odd (+0.16 ± 0.015, BEN 16; +0.15 ± 0.013, BEN 64); on partner's lead count +0.13, attitude +0.10; discards and trumps weaker. Opening leads match third-and-fifth 0.58 / 0.61 and fourth best 0.53 / 0.58 against 0.30 chance.
- Reading BEN's defenders (gain in nats per decision; in brackets the difference from Gen 8):

| Reader of BEN's defenders | BEN (16) | BEN (64) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Gen 8: the net on the card at 0.3 | +0.263 | +0.185 |
| The house net in full | +0.144 (-0.119 ± 0.064) | -0.024 (-0.208 ± 0.059) |
| Class term alone at 1 | +0.167 (-0.096 ± 0.052) | +0.001 (-0.184 ± 0.052) |
| Class at 1 + symmetric honesty | +0.281 (+0.017 ± 0.076) | +0.281 (+0.097 ± 0.073) |
| Class at 1 + emission tables | +0.474 (+0.211 ± 0.068) | +0.443 (+0.258 ± 0.064) |
| Class at 0.3 + emission tables | +0.425 (+0.162 ± 0.050) | +0.501 (+0.316 ± 0.048) |

- Symmetric honesty fails for BEN (+0.02, +0.10, within noise): it reads BEN's informative high card as weak evidence. The emission tables (P(lowest, highest, middle spot | parity) per signal; `signals.SignalModel(emission=...)`, new) are what work.
- Reading BEN's declarer play (house defender's decisions; Gen 8's 0.3 is the reference):

| BEN's declarer play read at | BEN (16) | BEN (64) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| factor 0 | +0.474 (-0.128 ± 0.020) | +0.441 (-0.110 ± 0.021) |
| factor 0.3 | +0.602 | +0.551 |
| factor 0.5 | +0.620 (+0.018 ± 0.012) | +0.552 (+0.001 ± 0.013) |
| factor 1 | +0.483 (-0.119 ± 0.043) | +0.360 (-0.191 ± 0.048) |

- Against the predictions: beats the house net at 0.3 and at 1, yes. Most of the gain from a factor per role and trick band, no: the factors were already right. Adapter on the policy net: not attempted.
- Evidence: prediction (true layout's log weight), held-out deal families.
- Sample: 300 held-out tables per BEN version (about 1,580 house declarer and 1,390 house defender decisions each), fitted on 2,319 and 2,918 tables; class 0.3 + emission minus Gen 8: +0.162 ± 0.050 (BEN 16), +0.316 ± 0.048 (BEN 64), 1 se clustered by table.
- Revisit if: an adapter on the policy net is affordable (box or long sandbox run), or E9-10's played boards disagree with the prediction.
- Outcome: **adopted** as the model of BEN for E9-10 (`configs/carding/ben16.json`, `ben64.json`; bot `r9b-32`). The reader now also learns emission tables from experience (`card.<key>.emit`, after 200 occasions), so a new player with BEN-like habits gets such a model without a fit.
- Data and code: `feature/gen9-signals-v2` at `8851bb3`, `reports/gen9-ben-reading-2026-11-12/`, `scripts/gen9_ben_reading.py`.
