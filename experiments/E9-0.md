## Hypothesis

Tilting the bot's layout weights toward the true layout by a set amount `d` (in nats, at every decision from trick 1) raises its result against the same bot untilted, roughly in proportion for small `d`, at about 0.3 to 0.4 IMPs a board per nat, flattening toward the double-dummy bound as `d` grows. The one point we have: Gen 8's reading from trick 1 raised the true layout's weight by about 1.1 nats over tricks 1 to 6 and won 0.40 IMPs a board against Gen 7 (board entries of 03:14 and 06:48 UTC, 12 Nov). Prediction, not a known value.

## Mechanism

The search plays the double-dummy best card on its weighted layouts. Weight moved onto the truth moves its choices toward the best card on the actual deal. This is the exchange rate for every reader in Gen 9: a reader is measured in nats offline, and this curve says what those nats buy.

## Primary metric and population

IMPs a board of a tilted `8p-32` against untilted `8p-32`, fork-free, fresh deals, at `d` in {0.5, 1, 2, 4} and with the deal known (the bound), 800 boards each; and the realized mean gain in the true layout's log weight per decision, which is what the x axis is. The tilt: the true layout is put in the pool or list if absent (at the pool's mean weight) and its weight multiplied by `exp(d)`. **Test-only bot: it is given the deal on purpose. It carries an `oracle` key that arena preparation must refuse.** Kind of evidence: played boards.

## Regression slices

Tilt only as declarer; only as defender. Tilt only at tricks 1 to 6; only from trick 7.
