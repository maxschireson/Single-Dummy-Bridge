## Hypothesis

For a player who bids a profile we hold except for one declared difference, the card's term applied on that difference alone (the profile's residual) reads the pair better than the profile alone, and costs nothing on the auctions the difference does not touch. Test case: the rule bidder's standard settings with a 14 to 16 no-trump, read as the 15 to 17 profile (by replay of the 15 to 17 bidder) with the declared card's 1NT rules as the residual. Prediction: on auctions that start 1NT or where 1NT was passed over (a balanced 14 or 17 opened otherwise), the residual recovers at least half of the gap between the wrong-range profile and the right bidder; on all other auctions the change is within 0.05 nats. Prediction, not a known value.

## Mechanism

This is the one case where "their card post-filters the net" should hold: the card says exactly where the player departs from the profile, and the term is applied nowhere else. The full card on a matching profile has been measured and makes the reading worse (-0.35 to -0.6 nats, 12 Nov), because the card's approximations are paid for on every call.

## Primary metric and population

Offline, by the method of E9-1: gain in the true layout's log weight a decision (leader before the lead, declarer at trick 1, pool 512), 800 decisions a row, readers paired on the same pools. Rows: the 15 to 17 profile by replay; the same plus the residual term; the right bidder by replay (the ceiling); the card alone. Split: auctions the no-trump range touches, and the rest.

## Regression slices

The same with the house Gen 6 net as the profile and a guest that bids Gen 6 except for opening 1NT on 14 to 16 (overridden by rule), if that is cheap to build. A threshold sweep for what counts as a residual context.
