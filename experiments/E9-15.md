## Hypothesis

For an opponent we hold many boards of, a signal expert fitted to that opponent (the probability of each spot among equivalent cards given the situation and the hand) reads its signals better than one emission table per kind of signal, because how honestly a player signals depends on the situation. For BEN, on E9-7's held-out tables: the expert adds +0.05 nats or more per house declarer decision over E9-7's tables (which added +0.16 and +0.32 over Gen 8); the effective honesty of BEN's count differs by a factor of two or more between situations; and reading the second round of a suit (the completed high-low), which the reader ignores today, gives more than half of the added gain. Predictions, not known values.

## Mechanism

Max, 12 Nov 12:29 UTC: "We should read opponents' signals for known opponents, for example BEN's count signals, with a level of honesty calibration. Ideally we would have per-opponent experts that calibrate honesty per situation." A defender signals when partner needs to know and not when declarer gains more, so one honesty per kind of signal averages situations where the card means a lot with situations where it means nothing. E9-7's table already shows the shape: on declarer's lead BEN 64 plays its highest spot 20% of the time from an even holding and 5% from an odd one; a high card says much, a low card little. "Even" there lumps a doubleton with four cards, and only the first round of a suit is read.

## Primary metric and population

BEN 16 and BEN 64 as defenders, E9-7's data and split by deal family (fit on four fifths, 300 held-out tables a version). An occasion: a BEN defender plays one of two or more equivalent spots (E9-3's classes) in a situation with a key.

Models, each fitted per opponent:
- M0: one symmetric honesty per key (today's default reader).
- M1: an emission table per key and parity (E9-7; the reference).
- M2: a finer table with partial pooling: key x exact length of the suit (2, 3, 4, 5 or more) x number of equivalent spots x whether the signaller holds an honour in the suit, each cell shrunk toward its M1 row by pseudo-counts. It is made of additive counts, so it lives in the experience store and any player gets one from its own boards.
- M3: the expert: a multinomial logistic model (or one hidden layer of 16) for the position of the card played (lowest, middle, highest) on the features below, fitted by maximum likelihood with a penalty toward M1.

Features. Public: the key, with two new ones for the second round of a suit the seat has already followed to (partner's lead, declarer's lead), where the feature is the first card's position; no-trump or suit contract; trump or side suit; led from declarer's hand, from dummy or by partner; the signaller's position in the trick; trick number; dummy's length in the suit; whether dummy holds a suit of five or more with no side entry (the case where count is needed); whether the trick is already decided when the signaller plays. From the layout: the signaller's exact length and honours in the suit, and its point band.

Reported, on held-out tables only:
1. Log-likelihood per occasion of the spot BEN played, M0 to M3, and a reliability table by situation (predicted against observed rate of the informative card).
2. **BEN's honesty by situation**: for each public cell with 200 or more occasions, the effective honesty (the symmetric honesty that carries the same information about the fact) with its interval. One table a version. This is what Max asked to see calibrated.
3. Reading gain per house declarer decision, in nats and, through G9-X2's bench, in tricks saved a board, for M1, M2 and M3 against Gen 8.
4. The same against the number of tables of experience (50, 200, 1,000, all): how many boards of an opponent before an expert beats the tables, and the tables beat one honesty.

## Regression slices

A model fitted on one BEN version read on the other. The house bots (who signal nothing): the expert must come out at honesty near zero in every cell, and change nothing (within 0.02 nats). Attitude and discards for BEN, where E9-7 found weaker signals.
