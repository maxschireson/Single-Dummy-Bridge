## Goal

Score Gen 9's readers by what p41's reading bench measures: the double-dummy cost on the true deal of the card the search would choose, against no reading, in tricks a board, with the deal as the unit of error. Take scripts/gen8_reading_bench.py from feature/gen8-p41-20261112 at bb1cd2c as a snapshot (one commit, to be dropped at the base swap; its report is reports/gen8-reading-by-net-2026-11-12 there). Add one stage: for the stored layouts of every decision, the log weight a Gen 9 player gives them (Gen9Player.play_log_likelihood from the decider's view, after meet() with a model; and for an auction reader the change replay_correction or read_calls makes against the scorer the pool was drawn by), stored as one more reading that the bench's report scores like a net. First readings, in this order: (1) the emission model of BEN against Gen 8's reading on BEN's held-out tables, both versions: the prediction for E9-10 in tricks and IMPs; (2) a declared scheme read at honesty 0.8 and at 0.5 against Gen 8, on the relabelled tables of E9-4 part 1; (3) with bill: the replayed house bidder against the scorer on house tables (E9-11's question).

## Done when

A PR into feature/gen9 (reviewer bill) with the snapshot, the new stage and a test on two tables; a report with, for each reading, nats and tricks saved a board side by side, by trick band and by declarer or defender deciding, with standard errors by deal; E9-10's and E9-4's files carry the tricks-based prediction before their box results are read. ## Context

Why: nats of the true layout's weight do not predict tricks (p41: v0 to v4.1 doubles the nats, +0.35 to +0.69, for +0.117 to +0.198 tricks a board; bill's pilots). From 12 Nov a Gen 9 reader is judged in tricks saved, nats beside it. p41's aimed-information table gives the rate: about 0.14 tricks a board per nat a decision aimed at every card, 0.27 aimed at honours, flat above about 0.54. Ask on box/coordination (my entry of 12:00 UTC) whether p41's stored pools for 530 house tables are published; if they are, reading (2) and (3) need no solving.
