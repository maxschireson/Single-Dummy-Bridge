## Goal

One run of the epoch runner on azure4 in which two players nobody has met arrive with what they declare, and everything Gen 9 is for happens in order with no code change after the run starts. This run is what Max reviews on Friday, so it comes before every other use of the box except E9-0.

**Cast.**
- `9a-32`: the Gen 9 bot: the shell (`bridge_harness/gen9.py`) with the card reader (`bidding_reader`), the replay reader for house bidders (`replay_reader`), the signal reader and experience on.
- `8p-32`: its control, the same engine with no player model.
- `7s-32` and two more house bots or anchors, as the capsule allows.
- `guest-a`, submitted before epoch 2: bids the rule bidder's precision settings; plays the cards with the Gen 8 prototype engine and upside-down count and attitude at honesty 0.8 (sam's carding tie-break). Its package declares its card (`rb-precision`) and its carding descriptor, with no honesty stated. The house holds its card, not its bidder: the replay reader must not be given the rule bidder for it.
- `guest-b`, submitted before epoch 4: bids a house bidder (Gen 5) and declares the standard card nearest to it (bill picks it from the distance matrix of G9-B4); plays standard signals at honesty 0 (its spots say nothing). This is the other path: a card that maps onto a profile we hold, and a declared scheme that is not followed.
- One house bot is retired before epoch 4 by a queued registry command.

**Sequence.** Five epochs. Epoch 1: 600 boards among the house players. Epochs 2 to 5: 1,200 boards each, of which 300 are `9a-32~guest-a` and 300 are `8p-32~guest-a` on the same deals (a fixed floor in the schedule, written in the rules file), and from epoch 4 the same floor for `guest-b` at 200 boards a pair; the scheduler places the rest. About 5,400 boards, 5 to 6 hours of the box.

**At a submission** the registry computes the newcomer's first model with `player_model.arrive(id, bidding=card, carding=descriptor, distances=...)`, the distances from `cards.match.card_distance` against every system we hold, and stores it with the package. **At every boundary** the experience snapshot freezes (G9-D7) and each bot is handed the other side's model with that snapshot. The shell then moves the profile probabilities by the `profile` statistic (`player_model.believed`), takes the play factor from `play.info`, and the readers take the off-card rates and the honesty from their statistics.

## Done when

On a `transfer/gen9-arrival-*` branch: the registry log, the five epoch manifests and records, the snapshots, and one page with these, each beside the prediction registered here before the run:

1. **The player model at the table.** `9a-32` against `guest-a` minus `8p-32` against `guest-a`, same deals, IMPs a board with its standard error, pooled over epochs 2 to 5 (1,200 boards a pair) and by epoch. Prediction: +0.4 or more pooled. Basis: E9-1 part 1 (the scorer reads precision auctions 5.8 nats worse than not reading them) and E9-4 part 1 (+0.7 nats a declarer decision at honesty 0.8). Not known: the rate of exchange between nats and IMPs (E9-0), and how much same deals reduce the error.
2. **What experience adds.** By epoch: the probability on the `card` profile for `guest-a` (prediction from E9-2: above 0.95 after epoch 2), the honesty estimate for `guest-a` (truth 0.8) and for `guest-b` (truth 0; prediction: below 0.2 after one epoch), the play factor chosen. And whether item 1's difference rises from epoch 2 to epochs 3 to 5 (prediction: it does, by less than its standard error at this size; reported whichever way it falls).
3. **The other path.** `9a-32` against `guest-b` minus `8p-32` against `guest-b`. Prediction: between -0.1 and +0.2 (the profile is one the house already reads, and there is nothing in its spots to read; the test is that a declared scheme that is not followed costs nothing once the honesty is learned).
4. **The arena.** For each arrival: the registry entry, the admission decision with its width, the newcomer's schedule share by epoch, ratings with intervals by epoch, and the state of the betting test for `9a-32` against `8p-32`.
5. **Replay.** One board of epoch 3 replayed from its manifest and the named snapshot, card for card.

Before the box run: a rehearsal on one machine, two epochs of 24 boards with `guest-a` arriving before the second, its records pushed, and a note to g9 with the bot entries and the rules file. g9 checks the wiring against the interfaces (this is a design review checkpoint) and releases the box run.
