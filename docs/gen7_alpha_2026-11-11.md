# Gen 7 alpha (11 November 2026)

**Status: packaged, not launched.** Branch `feature/gen7-alpha-20261111`, cut from `main` at
`b0909bb`. The plan file ships closed; three items in its `pending` list are Max's to settle
(the policy release, the match mix, the settings). Nothing here changes Gen 6 or a running
collector.

**What the alpha is for.** A low-volume cohort in the current arena to check that the Gen 7
architecture runs end to end, to measure what it costs, and to get first evidence on its choices
from play. It is not a strength claim. The fork-pair bench run while the tree was being built
(5,950 gap-fork pairs from trick 3, two-trick tree, two wide, 8 layouts, v1 net; rows not in the
repository) had the tree replacing the Gen 6 search's values at −0.011 ± 0.004 tricks per pair
overall and +0.016 ± 0.008 on pairs Gen 6 rated within 0.125 tricks. The alpha uses the tree only
in that second way.

Read [the prototype write-up](gen7_prototype_2026-11-11.md) first for the net, the play weights
and the measurements behind them. This page covers what was added to make an arena bot.

## The bot

A Gen 7 bot is its Gen 6 base bot (same bidder, lead net, hand-fit scorer, layout settings, sample
count) with `engine: gen7`, a policy net and a `gen7` block of settings. The opening lead and
every layout still come from Gen 6's machinery, and DDS still ranks the cards. The net is read in
four places; each is a switch.

| Where | Setting | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layout weights from the deal list on | `play_weight`, `play_floor`, `play_max_layouts`, `play_lead` | Each listed layout is weighted by how likely the hidden seats' plays were under the net |
| Exact ties | `tie_break` | `net`: the net's most probable card. `gen5`: the Gen 5 policy, as Gen 6 |
| Early tricks without search | `search_from_trick`, `net_sample` | For fast data tables only. 1 = always search |
| Tree search | `tree`, `tree_audit` | Below |

### The tree step

At a decision inside a tree stage, after the Gen 6 search has valued every legal card:

1. **Candidates.** Cards the search rates within `root_margin` tricks of its best, at most
   `root_cards`. Cards the solves cannot tell apart (same suit, same result in every solved
   layout) are one candidate, and the net's likeliest of them is the card searched. With one
   candidate the tree is not run.
2. **Layouts.** `layouts` of the decision's own solved layouts, drawn in proportion to weight.
3. **Search.** `tricks` tricks are played from each seat's own view before double dummy
   ([gen7_tree.py](../bridge_harness/gen7_tree.py)): another seat's card comes from the net
   given what that seat sees (`reply`, kept by `breadth` and `mass`), the bot's own later card
   by `own`.
4. **Value.** `estimate + trust × (tree value − double-dummy value on the same layouts)`. The
   tree corrects the search's estimate; it does not replace it. The best candidates stay
   eligible and the tie-break chooses among them.
5. **Budget.** A search that needs more than `max_solves` layout solves is dropped and the
   search's own card is played.

The tree's random draws come from a copy of the table's random stream. A tree with `trust` 0, or
with `tricks` 0, plays Gen 6's game card for card (tested).

### Settings

Every one is in the bot's spec, so every one is part of the bot's identity. `tree` is a list of
stages; a stage holds all of the keys below and applies from `from_trick` to `to_trick`. Stages
may not share a trick. Different depth, width, layouts or reply model at different tricks is a
second stage, not a code change. No `tree` entry means no tree.

| Key | Shipped | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `from_trick`, `to_trick` | 3, 13 | Tricks at which the stage applies. The tree needs dummy, so never the opening lead |
| `root_margin` | 0.125 | How far below the search's best a card may be and still be a candidate |
| `root_cards` | 3 | Most candidates |
| `trust` | 1.0 | Weight on the tree's correction |
| `max_solves` | 12000 | Solve budget per decision; 0 = none |
| `tricks` | 2 | Depth: to the end of this many tricks, counting the one in progress |
| `breadth`, `mass` | 3, 0.9 | Width below the root: most cards kept at a node, and the probability at which keeping stops |
| `layouts` | 8 | Layouts carried into the tree |
| `reply` | `net` | Another seat's card: `net`, `dd` (double dummy, as PIMC assumes) or `search` (a small search from that seat's view) |
| `reply_power`, `reply_gap` | 1.0, 99 | Sharpen the net's reply: probabilities to this power; drop cards the net rates this much worse |
| `own`, `own_breadth` | `best`, 0 | The bot's own later card: `best` over the layouts that still look alike, `net`, or `search`; and its width (0 = `breadth`) |
| `reply_layouts`, `reply_pool` | 8, 64 | Size of the stand-in searcher for `search` replies |

`tricks`, `breadth` and `layouts` are Max's target shape from the build session (two tricks, capped
at three, eight layouts, from trick 3). `root_margin` is the band the bench supported.
`max_solves` is about 1.6 times the 99th percentile measured below. `play_weight` 0.5 and
`tie_break` `net` are the prototype's defaults; neither has a measured effect on tricks yet.

### What a decision records

`estimates` are still the Gen 6 search's, so existing readers are unaffected. A decision in a
stage adds `gen7_tree`:

- `stage`, `consulted`, and when not consulted why (`candidates: 1`, or `over_budget`);
- `candidates`: for each, its `cards`, the card `searched`, `gap` to the search's best, the
  double-dummy value on the tree's layouts (`same_layouts`), the `tree` value, the `adjusted`
  value and whether it was `kept`;
- `gen6_cards`: the cards the search alone left eligible;
- the work: `layouts`, `lines`, `nodes`, `net_rows`, `solves`, `replies`, `seconds`.

`selected_pimc_gap` above zero means the tree moved the card off the search's best.

**The audit scores the tree.** With `tree_audit` on (the default), an audited bot names as its
reference card the card it would have played without the tree. The collector's ordinary audit
(a coin per decision, a shared compute budget, no code change) then plays that card out from the
same position whenever the two differ. Each such fork is one paired observation of the tree's
card against the no-tree card. `scripts/gen7_alpha_report.py` reads them.

## Launching

1. Settle the three `pending` items in `configs/round7-alpha.json`, empty the list, set
   `launch_enabled` true.
   - **Policy release.** `artifacts.gen7_policy` names a release folder, a file and its SHA-256.
     To use the retrained net, release it like v1 (parts, `restore.py`, a `manifest.json` with
     `scorer_sha256`) and change those three values. Preparation refuses a file that does not
     match the hash, and a release trained on a different hand-fit scorer from the base bot's.
     A net with a different input layout is a code change in `gen7_net.py`, not a config change.
   - **Match mix.** `pair_weights` ships with the alpha pair alone. Add the producer's other
     pairs to give the alpha a share of a cohort.
2. Drain the source run, then
   `python ops/prepare_gen7_alpha.py --source DRAINED_RUN --run NEW_RUN`.
3. `python ops/smoke_gen7.py --run NEW_RUN --out report.json`. It plays `--deals` boards of every
   scheduled Gen 7 pair and fails if a record does not add up or the tree is never consulted.
4. Start `ops/gen5_runtime.py --run NEW_RUN` as usual.
5. `python scripts/gen7_alpha_report.py --episodes NEW_RUN/episodes`.

Carried over from the source run, not set by the plan: tie-audit and fork settings (`audit`,
`ben_audit`, `gap_fork`), room seeding, bidding conditions, the corpus facts, and `generation` /
`variant`. The last two stay as the source run's because the exporter and report registration key
on them; the alpha is identified by its bot id, campaign and title. A producer's own state
(for example an allocation trigger) is not carried.

## Checked here

Machine: 2 cores, TensorFlow 2.21.0, NumPy 2.5.3, DDS built from `vendor/`. Policy v1.

- **Tests.** The whole suite passes: 226 tests, 1 skipped (a bidding-loop DDS integration test that
  needs `BRIDGE_DDS_LIBRARY` set). 44 are Gen 7's: 21 from the prototype, 15 and 8 new.
- **A real drained run.** The `arena-gen6-gen7-capture-feed` recovery capsule at `8833c62`
  (manifest `a76a39f1…`, 725 boards played, its own bidders and nets) was rebuilt locally and
  used as the source. Preparation froze the 22,508 unplayed deals; the Gen 7 bot differs from
  `6-32` only in engine, policy file, settings, role label and identity.
- **Preflight** through `gen5_runtime.play` on that cohort: passes.
- **Collector dry run.** `ops/gen5_runtime.py --workers 1` played 6 boards of `7a-32~6-32` and
  stopped on `STOP`: 12 root tables, 102 tables with forks, no failures, records validated and
  replayed by the runtime.

What those 6 boards show about cost and use (`gen7_alpha_report.py`):

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Gen 7 decisions in a stage (all tables) | 864 |
| Tree searched | 39% |
| Card moved off the search's best | 3.8% |
| Over the solve budget | 1 |
| Seconds per tree search | 1.0 mean, 4.5 at the 95th percentile, 12.4 worst |
| By trick | 2.9 s at tricks 3–4, 1.8 at 5–6, 1.0 at 7–9, 0.23 at 10–13 |
| Search time per root table, the two Gen 7 seats | 13.1 cpu-s |
| The two Gen 6 seats at the same tables | 3.3 cpu-s |
| Tree-against-no-tree forks | 37: the tree's card took more tricks in 2, fewer in 0, the same in 35 |

So the alpha's seats cost about four times Gen 6's. The match and fork figures are far too few to
read; they show the instruments work.

A separate 24-board run against the local Gen 6 on recorded contracts and leads
(`gen7_check.py match`, no solve budget, deals not restricted to the net's test split): 814
decisions in a stage, 41% searched, 6.5% moved; 1,121 solves per search, 3,570 at the 95th
percentile, 7,545 at the 99th, 15,325 worst; −0.42 ± 1.04 IMPs a board.

## Not done, and limits

- **Strength is unmeasured.** Nothing here says the alpha beats or matches Gen 6.
- **No BEN board was played** with a Gen 7 bot. The preflight skips BEN pairs.
- **Self-play pairs** (`6-32~6-32`) need a runtime with the self-play regime. The collectors'
  branches have it; `main` validates common and native only.
- **The dashboard.** The exporter and `gh-pages` were not touched. A new cohort has to be added
  to the dashboard's sources as the Gen 6 cohorts were.
- **Speed.** The tree is not optimised: a leaf batch solves about 1.2 layouts on average, so
  per-call overhead dominates.
- **Training on alpha tables.** `gen7_build_dataset.py` reads any decision with search
  estimates and tags a Gen 7 bot's as "other". Its gap targets are the Gen 6 estimates, and the
  card played may not be the one they rate best. Decide how to label these before training on
  them; the adjusted values are in `gen7_tree`.
- **The opening lead and tricks 1–2** are the Gen 6 search as shipped. `from_trick` can be
  lowered to 1 (after the lead); that was not timed.
- **`reply: search` and `own: search`** run (tested on small settings) but were not timed in the
  player.
- **The build session continues elsewhere.** `transfer/gen7-prototype-20261111` at `51e8478`
  plus that session's uncommitted tree and bench edits as of about 10:30 UTC are in this branch.
  Later work there has to be ported.
