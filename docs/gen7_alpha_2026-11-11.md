# Gen 7 alpha (11 November 2026)

**Later:** the bots, match mix, plans and launch steps for Gen 7 itself are in
[the Gen 7 notice](gen7_candidate_2026-11-11.md); its phase 1 was authorized on 12 November. This page remains the guide to the engine
and its settings; its shipped plan (`configs/round7-alpha.json`) is the alpha as first packaged,
with net v2.

**Status: packaged, not launched.** Branch `feature/gen7-alpha-20261111`, cut from `main` at
`b0909bb`. The plan file ships closed; the items in its `pending` list are Max's to settle (the
policy release, the match mix, the tree's tricks, the other settings). Nothing here changes Gen 6
or a running collector.

**What the alpha is for.** A low-volume cohort in the current arena to check that the Gen 7
architecture runs end to end, to measure what it costs, and to get first evidence on its choices
from play. It is not a strength claim.

**What the bench says about it.** [The prototype write-up](gen7_prototype_2026-11-11.md) scored
the tree on fork pairs with its values replacing the Gen 6 search's on 8 layouts (net v1). From
trick 3 on no tree beat the plain search: the two-trick tree was −0.011 ± 0.004 tricks a pair.
It was +0.016 ± 0.008 where Gen 6 rated the cards within 0.125 tricks, and weighted back to
tables the loss sat at tricks 7–13 (−0.27 ± 0.10 tricks a side) with tricks 3–6 at +0.11 ± 0.13.
The alpha follows both findings: the tree only chooses among the search's cards within 0.125,
and as shipped only at tricks 3–6. That combination itself (a correction added to the full
search, inside a margin) has not been benched, and the write-up asks for the bench on held-out
deals with each new net before any arena board. That has not been run for v2.

Read the prototype write-up first for the net, the play weights and those measurements. This
page covers what was added to make an arena bot.

## The bot

A Gen 7 bot is its Gen 6 base bot (same bidder, lead net, hand-fit scorer, layout settings, sample
count) with `engine: gen7`, a policy net and a `gen7` block of settings. The opening lead and
every layout still come from Gen 6's machinery, and DDS still ranks the cards. The net is read in
four places; each is a switch.

| Where | Setting | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layout weights from the deal list on | `play_weight`, `play_floor`, `play_max_layouts`, `play_lead` | Each listed layout is weighted by how likely the hidden seats' plays were under the net |
| Exact ties | `tie_break` | `net`: the net's most probable card. `gen5`: the Gen 5 policy, as Gen 6 |
| Early tricks without search | `search_from_trick`, `net_sample` | For fast data tables only. 1 = always search |
| Tree search | `tree`, `tree_audit` | Below |

### The tree step

At a decision inside a tree stage, after the Gen 6 search has valued every legal card:

1. **Candidates.** Cards the search rates within `root_margin` tricks of its best, at most
   `root_cards`. Cards the solves cannot tell apart (same suit, same result in every solved
   layout) are one candidate, and the net's likeliest of them is the card searched. With one
   candidate the tree is not run.
2. **Layouts.** `layouts` of the decision's own solved layouts, drawn in proportion to weight.
3. **Search.** `tricks` tricks are played from each seat's own view before double dummy
   ([gen7_tree.py](../bridge_harness/gen7_tree.py)): another seat's card comes from the net
   given what that seat sees (`reply`, kept by `breadth` and `mass`), the bot's own later card
   by `own`.
4. **Value.** `estimate + trust × (tree value − double-dummy value on the same layouts)`. The
   tree corrects the search's estimate; it does not replace it. The best candidates stay
   eligible and the tie-break chooses among them.
5. **Budget.** A search that needs more than `max_solves` layout solves is dropped and the
   search's own card is played.

The tree's random draws come from a copy of the table's random stream. With the other switches
off, a tree with `trust` 0, or with `tricks` 0, plays Gen 6's game card for card (tested).

### Settings

Every one is in the bot's spec, so every one is part of the bot's identity. `tree` is a list of
stages; a stage holds all of the keys below and applies from `from_trick` to `to_trick`. Stages
may not share a trick. Different depth, width, layouts or reply model at different tricks is a
second stage, not a code change. No `tree` entry means no tree.

| Key | Shipped | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `from_trick`, `to_trick` | 3, 6 | Tricks at which the stage applies. The tree needs dummy, so never the opening lead |
| `root_margin` | 0.125 | How far below the search's best a card may be and still be a candidate |
| `root_cards` | 3 | Most candidates |
| `trust` | 1.0 | Weight on the tree's correction |
| `max_solves` | 12000 | Solve budget per decision; 0 = none |
| `tricks` | 2 | Depth: to the end of this many tricks, counting the one in progress |
| `breadth`, `mass` | 3, 0.9 | Width below the root: most cards kept at a node, and the probability at which keeping stops |
| `layouts` | 8 | Layouts carried into the tree |
| `reply` | `net` | Another seat's card: `net`, `dd` (double dummy, as PIMC assumes) or `search` (a small search from that seat's view) |
| `reply_power`, `reply_gap` | 1.0, 99 | Sharpen the net's reply: probabilities to this power; drop cards the net rates this much worse |
| `own`, `own_breadth` | `best`, 0 | The bot's own later card: `best` over the layouts that still look alike, `net`, or `search`; and its width (0 = `breadth`) |
| `reply_layouts`, `reply_pool` | 8, 64 | Size of the stand-in searcher for `search` replies |
| `plain` | 0 | 1: the tree deals the hidden hands' low cards (2 to 9) by a fixed rule and counts cards that do the same job as one. The double-dummy value on the same layouts is then the tree's own, since its layouts are no longer quite the search's |
| `path`, `spread`, `path_solves` | 0, 0, 0 | The stake rule, off at 0; see below |

**The stake rule** (added 11 November, late; off in the shipped spec). With `path` above zero the
tree is no longer uniform. A node is searched on only while (its share of the search) x (IMPs a
trick is worth at this decision) is at least `path`, and a card is kept at a node only while the
cards before it leave that much uncovered. A node's share is 1 at each candidate and is
multiplied by the probability of each card on the way down. So a line of clear plays runs deep, a
close call is wide but shallow below, and a decision where a trick is an overtrick gets almost no
search. `tricks`, `breadth` and `mass` are then upper limits, and a node cut short is solved
double dummy where it stands. The stake comes from every layout the Gen 6 search solved: the IMPs
between declarer's double-dummy result in a layout and one trick fewer (declaring) or one more
(defending), averaged with the layouts' weights, plus `spread` of their standard deviations.
`path_solves` turns what is left of one candidate's subtree into leaves once it has solved that
many layouts, so a costly decision degrades instead of being dropped by `max_solves`. With a
vanishing `path` the tree equals the uniform tree exactly, and with a huge one it is the search
itself (tested in the prototype's `tests/test_gen7.py`).

`tricks`, `breadth` and `layouts` are Max's target shape from the build session (two tricks, capped
at three, eight layouts, from trick 3). `to_trick` 6 and `root_margin` 0.125 come from the bench
as above; set `to_trick` to 13 for the tree from trick 3 to the end. Three cards a seat was not
established either way on the bench (351 pairs). `max_solves` is about twice the 99th percentile of solves measured below. `play_weight` 0.5 and
`tie_break` `net` are the prototype's defaults; neither has a measured effect on tricks yet.

### What a decision records

`estimates` are still the Gen 6 search's, so existing readers are unaffected. A decision in a
stage adds `gen7_tree`:

- `stage`, `consulted`, and when not consulted why (`candidates: 1`, or `over_budget`);
- `candidates`: for each, its `cards`, the card `searched`, `gap` to the search's best, the
  double-dummy value on the tree's layouts (`same_layouts`), the `tree` value, the `adjusted`
  value and whether it was `kept`;
- `gen6_cards`: the cards the search alone left eligible;
- the work: `layouts`, `lines`, `nodes`, `net_rows`, `solves`, `replies`, `seconds`.

`selected_pimc_gap` above zero means the tree moved the card off the search's best.

**The audit scores the tree.** With `tree_audit` on (the default), an audited bot names as its
reference card the card it would have played without the tree. The collector's ordinary audit
(a coin per decision, a shared compute budget, no code change) then plays that card out from the
same position whenever the two differ. Each such fork is one paired observation of the tree's
card against the no-tree card. `scripts/gen7_alpha_report.py` reads them.

## Launching

1. Settle the `pending` items in `configs/round7-alpha.json`, empty the list, set
   `launch_enabled` true.
   - **Policy release.** `artifacts.gen7_policy` names a release folder, a file and its SHA-256;
     it ships pointing at v2. For a later net, release it the same way (parts, `restore.py`, a
     `manifest.json` with `scorer_sha256`) and change those three values. Preparation refuses a
     file that does not match the hash, and a release trained on a different hand-fit scorer from
     the base bot's. A net with a different input layout is a code change in `gen7_net.py`, not a
     config change.
   - **Match mix.** `pair_weights` ships with the alpha pair alone. Add the producer's other
     pairs to give the alpha a share of a cohort.
   - **Tree tricks.** `to_trick` 6 as shipped, 13 for the tree from trick 3 to the end. A second
     stage gives later tricks their own depth, width and layouts.
2. Drain the source run, then
   `python ops/prepare_gen7_alpha.py --source DRAINED_RUN --run NEW_RUN`.
3. `python ops/smoke_gen7.py --run NEW_RUN --out report.json`. It plays `--deals` boards of every
   scheduled Gen 7 pair and fails if a record does not add up or the tree is never consulted.
4. Start `ops/gen5_runtime.py --run NEW_RUN` as usual.
5. `python scripts/gen7_alpha_report.py --episodes NEW_RUN/episodes`.

Carried over from the source run, not set by the plan: tie-audit and fork settings (`audit`,
`ben_audit`, `gap_fork`), room seeding, bidding conditions, the corpus facts, and `generation` /
`variant`. The last two stay as the source run's because the exporter and report registration key
on them; the alpha is identified by its bot id, campaign and title. A producer's own state
(for example an allocation trigger) is not carried.

`ops/gen7-fast-selfplay-cloud-20261111` at `3364f4a`. Its code applies as a patch to
`codex/gen6-gen7-capture-20261111` at `b81d674`; the two documentation-index edits do not, because

touch, as `main` itself does. On the merged first of those, with gap forks on, the Gen 6, Gen 7,
gap-fork and launch tests pass (101) and the preflight passes on a prepared run.
Gap forks are drawn at Gen 7 decisions there with no change (one board probed: 5 of its 11 gap forks
were at the Gen 7 bot's decisions). Their `estimated_gap` is the forced card's gap to the search's
best, as always; where the tree moved the card, the card it is played against has a gap of its own
(`selected_pimc_gap`). A gap fork never forces the search's best card, so only the tree audit
compares the tree's card with it.

## Checked here

Machine: 2 cores, TensorFlow 2.21.0, NumPy 2.5.3, DDS built from `vendor/`. Policy v2 and the plan
as shipped (tree at tricks 3–6) unless stated.

- **Tests.** The whole suite passes: 228 tests, 1 skipped (a bidding-loop DDS integration test that
  needs `BRIDGE_DDS_LIBRARY` set). 46 are Gen 7's: 23 from the prototype, 15 and 8 new.
- **A real drained run.** The `arena-gen6-gen7-capture-feed` recovery capsule at `8833c62`
  (manifest `a76a39f1…`, 725 boards played, its own bidders and nets) was rebuilt locally and
  used as the source. Preparation froze the 22,508 unplayed deals; the Gen 7 bot differs from
  `6-32` only in engine, policy file, settings, role label and identity.
- **Preflight** through `gen5_runtime.play` on that cohort: passes (3 boards).
- **Collector dry run.** `ops/gen5_runtime.py --workers 1` played 14 boards of `7a-32~6-32` and
  stopped on `STOP`: 28 root tables, 288 tables with forks, no failures, every record validated
  and replayed by the runtime.

What those 14 boards show about cost and use (`gen7_alpha_report.py`):

So with the tree at tricks 3–6 the alpha's seats cost a little over twice Gen 6's. The match and
fork figures are far too few to read; they show the instruments work.

A separate 30-board run against the local Gen 6 on recorded contracts and leads
(`gen7_check.py match`, no solve budget, deals not restricted to the net's held-out deals): 411
decisions in the stage, 39% searched, 12% moved; 1,258 solves per search, 3,233 at the 95th
percentile, 5,498 at the 99th, 7,030 worst; −0.30 ± 0.88 IMPs a board.

**With the stage through trick 13** (net v1, earlier in the same session): a 6-board collector dry
run had 39% of 864 decisions searched and 3.8% moved, at 1.0 s per search (2.9 s at tricks 3–4,
1.8 at 5–6, 1.0 at 7–9, 0.23 at 10–13), and the two Gen 7 seats cost 13.1 cpu-s per root table
against 3.3 for the two Gen 6 seats, about four times. A 24-board run without a budget reached
7,545 solves at the 99th percentile and 15,325 at worst.

## Not done, and limits

- **Strength is unmeasured.** Nothing here says the alpha beats or matches Gen 6.
- **No BEN board was played** with a Gen 7 bot. The preflight skips BEN pairs.
- **Self-play pairs** (`6-32~6-32`) need a runtime with the self-play regime. The collectors'
  branches have it; `main` validates common and native only.
- **The dashboard.** The exporter and `gh-pages` were not touched. A new cohort has to be added
  to the dashboard's sources as the Gen 6 cohorts were.
- **Speed.** The tree is not optimised: a leaf batch solves about 1.2 layouts on average, so
  per-call overhead dominates.
- **Training on alpha tables.** `gen7_build_dataset.py` reads any decision with search
  estimates and tags a Gen 7 bot's as "other". Its gap targets are the Gen 6 estimates, and the
  card played may not be the one they rate best. Decide how to label these before training on
  them; the adjusted values are in `gen7_tree`.
- **The opening lead and tricks 1–2** are the Gen 6 search as shipped. `from_trick` can be
  lowered to 1 (after the lead); that was not timed.
- **`reply: search` and `own: search`** run (tested on small settings) but were not timed in the
  player.
- **The bench gate.** The prototype write-up asks for the fork-pair bench on held-out deals with
  each new net before any arena board (`gen7_bench.py --splits 1,2 --from-trick 3 --to-trick 6`;
  its `mix_2` on the pairs within 0.125 and the tie pairs is this bot's rule). Not run here.
- **The build session continues elsewhere.** `transfer/gen7-prototype-20261111` is ported here
  commit for commit up to `dd23b5e` (11:10 UTC). Later work there has to be ported.
